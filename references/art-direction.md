# Independent art direction

Make every issue feel intentionally authored rather than generically AI-stylized.

- Establish one clear visual thesis in a short phrase, such as `quiet geometric domestic portrait` or `single-ink natural-history relief print`.
- Use one coherent mark grammar: uniform clear lines, broad carved cuts, dry brush washes, flat geometric planes, or another scheme-specific system. Do not mix unrelated rendering languages.
- Limit the palette to 2–5 deliberate colors derived from the source. Reserve the strongest contrast for the subject and required postal text.
- Preserve negative space and one dominant focal point. Simplify incidental background objects instead of describing everything.
- Introduce believable medium-specific imperfections: slight ink spread, uneven pressure, paper tooth, screen misregistration, or restrained plate wear. Never apply random grunge uniformly.
- Keep unaged concepts visibly fresh: use clean paper white, crisp tonal separation, and purposeful medium texture. Do not add yellowing, foxing, faded borders, uniform scratches, or archival patina unless the user explicitly requests a historic or aged object.
- Avoid common synthetic-image signals: glossy plastic gradients, over-smoothed forms, indiscriminate hyper-detail, inconsistent line weight, arbitrary texture, excessive glow, cinematic 3D lighting, crowded decorative filler, pseudo-lettering, and duplicated anatomy.
- Request exact required text only and remove all optional copy. Regenerate when country or postal marking is misspelled.
- Create an original visual voice from broad art and print principles. Do not imitate a living artist or reproduce a protected character, artwork, or postal issue.
