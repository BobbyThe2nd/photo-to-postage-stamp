# Blue-pencil field sketch

Use `SCHEME_BLUE_PENCIL_FIELD_SKETCH` to make a postal artifact feel observed, drawn, corrected, and human rather than digitally filtered.

## Visual thesis

Build an intimate field note in which the act of looking remains visible. Preserve the subject's key identifying silhouette, anatomy, pose, and spatial relationship before introducing simplification. The finished image should feel discovered through drawing, not traced from a photograph.

## Mark grammar

- Begin with very pale graphite axes, ellipses, proportion boxes, and gesture lines.
- Find the form with two or three slightly displaced blue-gray contours instead of one perfect outline.
- Let some contours overshoot, stop early, remain open, or fade into the paper.
- Vary pressure within a stroke. Use a few dark, confident lines only where the form resolves: eye, jaw, fin root, hull, branch joint, wheel, hand, or another subject-specific anchor.
- Add sparse directional hatching that follows volume. Leave most tonal areas unfilled.
- Include restrained erasure ghosts, fingertip smudges, and corrections. Keep them local and purposeful.
- Draw repeated studies with genuine differences in angle, scale, construction, or completion; never duplicate one drawing mechanically.

## Material and palette

- Use clean warm-ivory, cream, or very pale gray uncoated drawing paper.
- Use blue-gray colored pencil as the dominant mark, with pale graphite underneath.
- Allow one tiny rust-red, faded vermilion, or source-derived accent when it improves hierarchy.
- Show subtle paper tooth without yellowing, foxing, scratches, or uniform noise.

## Stamp issue behavior

- **Integrated souvenir sheet:** draw one continuous field-study scene across the sheet. The perforated stamp selects or resolves one part of that scene; do not repeat the same complete image inside and outside the stamp.
- **Field-studies pane:** keep the scheme consistent. Use different subjects, crops, poses, viewpoints, or stages from gesture to resolved contour in the individual stamps.
- **Repeated definitive pane:** repeat one identical, fully resolved stamp. Use the remaining margin for a clearly separate construction progression or scale study.
- Keep the default `PUNCHED_HOLE_CONNECTOR_CUT` perforations, postal hierarchy, and required lettering crisp enough to contrast with the exploratory drawing. Construction lines may approach but must not obscure required text.

## Postcard behavior

- Use the real 148:100 landscape proportion for both sides.
- Recompose the front as one quiet observational scene with generous negative space; do not paste or enlarge a stamp.
- Use one primary title and at most one much smaller secondary line.
- Keep the back functional and mostly empty: divider, stamp box, and three or four address lines, plus one small open-contour motif.
- In a social presentation poster, keep the equal-size front and back dominant over a subordinate working sheet containing gesture-to-contour progression, component studies, alternate crops, arrows, pressure tests, and short meaningful notes.

## Prompt lock

Include this language when the scheme is selected:

> observational blue-pencil field sketch on clean warm-ivory drawing paper; pale graphite construction underneath; repeated searching blue-gray contours; open edges, overshoots, local erasure ghosts, varied hand pressure, sparse volume-following hatching, and a few darker decisive resolving lines; preserve the source-identifying silhouette and anatomy; show genuine drawing decisions rather than a photo filter

## Reject and regenerate when

- the result looks like a grayscale or blue-tinted photo filter;
- every contour is equally dark, clean, closed, or vector-like;
- straight edges look ruler-drawn unless the subject structurally requires them;
- dots, grit, stippling, dense crosshatching, or etched detail become the dominant texture;
- construction marks are decorative overlays unrelated to the final form;
- the paper looks artificially antique or distressed;
- the main subject becomes less recognizable than the source;
- pseudo-handwriting, illegible notes, or excessive annotations compete with the postal artifact.

## Final inspection

At full resolution, confirm that the subject remains immediately identifiable, the searching contours are visibly hand-varied, the corrections relate to real construction decisions, negative space remains calm, perforations are complete, required postal text is exact and unobstructed, and the postcard front and back have identical dimensions.
