# Calm solid-wash doodle

Use this style to evolve flat-color doodle illustration into a quieter, more spatial, and more authored image without turning it into ink wash or a generic watercolor filter.

## Core construction

Build the image in this order:

1. Identify two or three structural anchors that make the subject recognizable: a roof rhythm, tree silhouette, chair grid, boat hull, face profile, animal anatomy, or other source-specific geometry.
2. Draw those anchors with direct water-based felt-tip or water-marker lines. Allow hand pressure, slight overshoot, open joins, and minor registration drift. Keep curves alive; never trace with ruler-straight vector precision.
3. Group the remaining source information into a few calm opaque color planes. Preserve large shapes and spatial rhythm instead of describing every object.
4. Add one or two broad translucent watercolor atmosphere fields only where they clarify light, air, snow, water, distance, or a shared ground plane.
5. Recover a few hard edges and dark accents at the focal anchor. Let peripheral edges soften or disappear into paper.

## Material balance

Treat these proportions as a compositional guide, not a mechanical quota:

- 55–70% visible paper or quiet negative space;
- 20–35% opaque solid-color planes;
- 5–15% broad translucent wash;
- 3–8% dark marker anchors and lettering.

Paper white is an active color. Use it to describe snow, light, sky, empty walls, pauses, and separation between shapes.

## Edge hierarchy

Use three distinct edge behaviors:

- **Hard dry-marker edge:** identity, anatomy, architecture, functional joints, and focal details.
- **Slightly irregular solid-color edge:** object planes, clothing, roofs, furniture, foliage masses, and signs.
- **Soft lost wash edge:** distance, atmosphere, reflected light, water, weather, and the outer boundary of the composed image.

Do not outline every plane. A readable solid-color shape can meet paper or another shape without a black contour.

## Color system

- Choose one muted dominant temperature family, one quiet counter-temperature, one small accent, and paper white.
- Keep saturation controlled. Reserve the brightest color for one focal roof, garment, vehicle, fruit, sign, or other source-derived anchor.
- Use transparent overlap to create a third tone instead of adding many unrelated colors.
- Prefer clear chromatic dilution over gray or black ink diffusion.

## Detail and perspective

- Preserve the source's key perspective and rhythm, but simplify repeated detail into a visual cadence.
- Keep one or two semantic anchors crisp. Let secondary windows, leaves, people, tables, or waves become compact signs.
- Use tiny figures or objects to establish scale only when they exist in the source or are clearly requested; do not invent a narrative crowd.
- Preserve identifying geometry before stylizing. Do not distort subjects until they become generic symbols.

## Lettering

Use loose hand-drawn water-marker or felt-tip lettering for the title and short note. Let baseline, spacing, and stroke weight vary slightly, but keep every word readable. Use one primary phrase and at most one smaller secondary thought. Do not use elegant brush calligraphy, commercial script fonts, dense typesetting, or pseudo-language.

## Prohibitions

Never use:

- ink-wash or sumi-e diffusion;
- stippling, dotted pigment buildup, granular speckles, mottled clusters, scab-like blooms, or dense repeated texture;
- uniformly blurry watercolor-filter rendering;
- black outlines around every object;
- glossy vector fills, airbrushed gradients, or perfectly straight CAD-like contours;
- copied captions, watermarks, QR codes, signatures, or ready-made text from reference images.

## Postcard application

On the front, keep one dominant scene or relationship and generous quiet paper. On the back, carry over only one marker emblem, one short colored edge, or one small solid-color motif. On the design-process poster, show the progression `source geometry → marker structure → color-plane grouping → limited wash atmosphere → final card` using pale working drawings behind the equal-sized front and back.
