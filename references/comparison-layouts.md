# Comparison-sheet layouts

Use these layouts for a final original-versus-stamp page. Borrow broad visual principles from editorial catalogues, museum field guides, and identity studies; never reproduce a supplied reference's logo, text, interface, or distinctive artwork.

## Shared rules

- Use a 3:5 portrait cream, white, or subdued color-field page with a fine modular grid.
- Keep both the original photo and the finished stamp fully visible. Make one the focal artifact and the other a clearly framed comparison artifact.
- When using a right-side auxiliary-image column, make each auxiliary image no more than 10% of the page area and keep the full column below 20%; the main stamp must remain the clear focal artifact.
- Let the source subject determine supplementary visuals: a bird can have feather/footprint/shoreline marks; architecture can have elevation lines; a person can have silhouette or clothing-color studies.
- Use only verified facts. When factual metadata is unavailable, label it `LOCATION: UNVERIFIED` or omit the field.
- Limit handwritten notes to 2–4 short phrases. Use clear editorial typography for all required stamp text.

## A. Specimen dossier

Place a large stamp as the hero at center-right. Set the original photo in a smaller framed source window at upper-left or lower-left. Connect them with fine numbered leader lines, a subject contour sketch, palette chips, and a compact metadata strip. Best for animals, plants, objects, and found imagery.

## B. Field-guide plate

Place the original photo as a dominant vertical crop along one side. Float the stamp partly into the open field on the opposite side without overlap. Add restrained identification arrows, an enlarged subject detail, and a small hand-drawn silhouette or diagram. Best for nature and landscape subjects.

## C. Construction study

Use a visible geometric construction grid behind the artifacts. Place the original and stamp on an offset diagonal, then add a simplified contour or proportion study and 2–3 palette/material samples. Use only when the subject has strong geometry, structure, or graphic form.

## D. Editorial archive

Use generous negative space and a single strong color block derived from the image. Present the original as an archival print or contact-sheet card, with the stamp as a pinned, taped, or mounted artifact beside it. Add one small caption block and one loose hand-drawn element. Best for portraits, fashion, and poetic scenes.

## Prompt checklist

State the selected layout by name. Specify `asymmetric research-board composition`, `visible modular grid`, `source photo preserved unchanged`, and `stamp fully visible`. Name the exact supporting marks and their safe locations. Explicitly request no dashboard chrome, fake UI, unreadable foreign text, invented charts, watermarks, or clutter.
