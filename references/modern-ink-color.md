# Modern point-line-plane ink and color

Use this reference whenever `SCHEME_INK_WASH` is selected. Build an original contemporary ink-and-color language informed by the broad point-line-plane principles associated with Wu Guanzhong's work. Do not reproduce a specific painting, composition, signature, seal, inscription, or recognizable motif.

## Visual thesis

Treat the subject as a rhythm of abstract structure and recognizable evidence rather than a conventionally rendered scene. Choose one concise thesis such as `coarse black grazing forms across a low-gray field` or `scattered cattle as colored punctuation in open grassland`.

## Point, line, and plane

- **Point:** Use a small number of high-chroma dots or short dabs as visual punctuation. Derive 2–4 accents from the source, such as turquoise, leaf green, vermilion, warm yellow, or pink. Cluster some points and leave others isolated; never scatter confetti uniformly.
- **Line:** Give line weight a clear hierarchy. Draw the main subject with coarse, weight-bearing burnt-ink dry-brush contours: broad pressure, frayed edges, blunt turns, broken fibers, and visible resistance against the paper. Use medium-weight strokes for the horizon and only sparse hairlines for incidental grass or spatial connectors. Let lines show speed, pauses, and abrupt pressure changes, but never make the subject outline uniformly thin, soft, elegant, or willow-leaf-like. Avoid mechanical outlines and decorative filigree.
- **Plane:** Anchor the composition with 1–3 decisive black, white, or gray masses plus translucent color washes. Use flat structural planes to simplify the scene rather than shading objects volumetrically.

Make points, lines, and planes interact: a black plane may become a cow flank or distant ridge; a wandering line may become a back, path, or horizon; colored points may suggest flowers, windows, or grazing marks without literal over-description.

## Composition

- Preserve large areas of clean paper white or very pale wash.
- Establish asymmetric balance and one dominant structural rhythm.
- Alternate dense and empty zones; do not fill every area with texture.
- Simplify incidental detail into a few marks. Keep the main subject readable from thumbnail size.
- Use cropping, scale shifts, and interrupted contours intentionally, but keep required postal text and perforations fully visible.

## Color and material

- Start from fresh neutral white paper, not cream, tan, or artificially aged stock.
- Use black, charcoal, graphite gray, smoke gray, and cool-to-neutral pale gray as the structural base.
- Render broad grass or ground planes primarily in layered low-key gray ink: cool gray, smoke gray, blue-gray, or restrained gray-green. Keep chroma low and tonal separation deliberate.
- Use celadon, turquoise, or mineral green only as a minor translucent undertone or small accent, never as the dominant field color.
- Reserve saturated accents for small points; they should feel luminous against black and white.
- Show credible ink behavior: wet blooms in selected areas, dry-brush drag, variable absorption, and occasional crisp opaque strokes. Keep the print clean and contemporary.

## Stamp application

- Make postal text part of the black structural rhythm without sacrificing exact spelling or legibility.
- Keep perforations clean and bright. Use only a faint cancellation mark.
- For integrated souvenir sheets, continue every wash, line, and structural plane seamlessly through the perforated boundary. Never repeat the scene inside the stamp or place a separate white panel behind it.
- For panes, maintain one coherent point-line-plane grammar across all stamps while varying density, crop, or accent placement.

## Avoid

- Generic monochrome misty-mountain imagery
- Uniform gray wash or timid pastel haze
- Sepia, yellowed rice paper, foxing, distressed borders, faded ink, or antique postcard effects
- Decorative pseudo-calligraphy, invented seals, signatures, or copied inscriptions
- Dense literal detail, photographic modeling, glossy gradients, or cinematic lighting
- Random splatter without compositional purpose
- Mint-green, celadon, or turquoise grass dominating the image
- Uniformly thin, graceful, soft, or willow-like subject contours
- Smooth digital outlines without dry-brush tooth, broken fibers, or pressure variation
- Direct copying of any reference artwork
